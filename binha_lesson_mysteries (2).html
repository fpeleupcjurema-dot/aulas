🔍 Mini-Mysteries

An English lesson for Binha · Level B1–B2 · Topic: everyday curiosities & historical puzzles

Warm-up

Welcome, detective!

Today you will solve small mysteries, make guesses, and defend your opinions. Speak out loud after every activity, and don't worry about mistakes!

Warm-up questions

1. What is the biggest mystery you have ever tried to solve, big or small?
2. Do you enjoy not knowing the answer, or does it bother you? Why?
3. Which is more interesting to you: a mystery from the past, or a mystery that science hasn't solved yet?
Useful language:
What really fascinates me is…I can't stand it when…I'd say that…To be honest,…
Vocabulary matching

Detective language

For each expression, choose the correct meaning from two options. Then use each one in your own sentence out loud.

Say it: Pick three expressions and make a sentence about your own life or work.
Guessing game

What is it?

Read one clue at a time. Try to guess the mystery object as early as you can. Fewer clues = better detective!

Your guess

Speak: Explain which clue helped you most. Then choose your own object and give 3 clues to your teacher.
It might be…It can't be… because…This clue helped me.
Mystery · problem solving

The ghost ship

In 1872 a ship called the Mary Celeste was found alone in the Atlantic Ocean. Reveal the evidence step by step.

Part A · Guessing (grammar: must / might / can't)

Choose the best option for each sentence.

Part B · What is your theory?

There is no single correct answer. Choose the theory you find most convincing.

Speak:
  • Say what you think happened. Use might, must or can't and give a reason with because.
  • Why is it hard to be sure about something from the past?
  • Optional challenge: If you had a time machine, what would you want to find out?
I think… because…Maybe…I'm not sure, but…It's unlikely that…
Agree or disagree

Mystery or explanation?

Choose your position, then explain it. Disagreeing politely is a skill!

Useful expressions:
I agree because…I don't agree because…In my opinion…It depends.That's true, but…
Would you rather…?

Choose your mystery

Pick one option, then answer the follow-up questions.

Your speaking challenge

Prepare for 2 minutes, then speak for 2–3 minutes without reading.

Structure your talk:
  • Introduce the mystery or situation.
  • Give the evidence or facts.
  • Give two possible explanations (use might / must / can't).
  • Say which you believe and why.

  • Timing (55 min): warm-up 5 · vocab 8 · guessing 7 · ghost ship 15 · agree/disagree 8 · would you rather 7 · final 5+.
  • Vocabulary: each word has two options and feedback appears immediately. Definitions are in the review list on the last tab (hover or tap). Ask her to use each expression in a sentence.
  • Guess: a mirror (accept "mirror"/"mirrors").
  • Modals key (present, for guessing): 1 must · 2 can't · 3 might · 4 might.
  • Mary Celeste facts: found Dec 1872 by the Dei Gratia; sails partly set; lifeboat missing; cargo of alcohol mostly intact (a few barrels empty); no clear sign of violence. The fate of the crew is still unknown. All theories are speculation.
  • Correction focus: must / might / can't for guessing, plus because to give reasons. Past forms (must have gone) are optional for a stronger student. Note errors while she speaks; give feedback at the end.
  • Extension: ask her to describe an unsolved mystery from nature or bioacoustics (e.g. a strange animal sound) as a light link to her work.
